What a patient says should reach the clinician intact.

Eulabic builds software that carries a patient’s own words to the people caring for them. It adds nothing, and it leaves the judgment to the clinician.

The first product, HeardAhead, is a prototype. Every patient shown on this page is made up.

HeardAhead is a brief the clinician reads before a checkup. The patient answers the checkup questions at home. The clinician opens one page before walking in, so the patient is heard ahead of the visit.

How it works

  1. The patient answers at home

    The checkup questions, one at a time, on a phone or a computer. A family member can help, and the brief says so when one did.

  2. An AI asks a few follow-up questions

    They are plain questions about what the patient already wrote. The page checks each one before the patient sees it. The AI is Claude, a model made by Anthropic.

  3. The page builds the brief and checks it

    Flags and scores come from fixed rules. Each sentence the AI writes is compared with the patient’s answers, and a sentence that cannot be traced to them is removed.

  4. The clinician opens one page

    What the patient wants to talk about comes first. Anything written by AI is labeled, and the patient’s own words are one tap away.

Ground rules

  • No real patient information, anywhere. Every patient in the prototype is made up.
  • The AI asks and reports. It does not diagnose, and it does not give advice.
  • A qualified clinician judges anything clinical.
  • Text written by AI is labeled, and every sentence points to the answer it came from.
  • If a patient writes about harming themselves, the AI turns off for that patient and the page shows a 911 and 988 message.
  • The limits are written down where the clinician can read them.

What comes next

None of this is built yet.

  • Question sets for other checkups: adults under 65, new patients, long-term conditions, and visits after a hospital stay.
  • A draft chart note that the clinician edits and signs.
  • What one doctor said, carried to the next one.
  • A plain recap after the visit, for the patient and family.
  • Other languages, with the patient’s original words kept.

Each of these needs real clinic records and privacy safeguards before it goes near a real patient. That work comes first.
